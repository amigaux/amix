# M-GEN2 — `kbootset commit` time, bench upper bound (2026-10-26)

- **Measurement:** M-GEN2 (90 §2): `kbootset commit` time on a 68030 and a
  68060.
- **Class:** bench, upper bound for the CPU. The disk-inclusive figures and
  the 68060 figure are metal (S2-47).
- **Recipe:** `tests/bench/README.md` "M-GEN2": one boot, three passes of
  `build`, `/usr/bin/time kbootset commit`, `mark-bad`, then a timed
  `check`.
- **Inputs:**
  - the box kbootset `9cf75c1c…` (amix-dlm d64bad7);
  - k30: the A4091 68030 vehicle on the cycle-exact 25 MHz 68030 with a
    68882, 8 MiB of fast memory; the stand-in package of K030-U
    (1 267 045 bytes, brel, `bss=344915`); the box facts on the command
    line (`--line 30 --bootdev zorro:0202:54 --rootdev zorro:0202:54
    --rootfs ufs --mem 8388608`); a 2 MiB boot slice;
  - k46: the kinfo vehicle (KD060-Z) on the 68060 model; the stand-in of
    the same kernel (1 998 273 bytes); a 4 MiB store; `build` with no
    options.
- **Evidence:** the workspace's
  `tmp/2026-10-26-s2/m-gen2/{k30b/N40,k46/N36}/06-serial.log`, the cell
  scripts `mgen2.sh` and `mgen2-box.sh` beside them.

`/usr/bin/time`, in seconds. Each commit wrote the kernel raw (a disk

"marked bad and collected".

## 2. What it means

- **The k30 figure is the upper bound for the CPU:** about 17 s for a
  commit of a k30 kernel on the slowest supported machine. The trigger runs
  it once per kernel-space transaction, and apkg says a power cycle
  follows, so it costs nothing at boot.
- **It is not a bound for the disk.** The emulated disk answers faster than
  a real SCSI disk. A commit writes the kernel once and reads it back once:
  about 2.5 MB of transfer for k30 and 4 MB for k46.
- **The 68060 model runs at the emulator's speed,** so its 6.4 s is for the
  record only, as M-SIG2's 68060 figure was.

**Finding: kbootset hashes more slowly than M-SIG2 predicts.**
- A commit computes one SHA-256 and two CRC-32s over the kernel, and
  compares the read-back. M-SIG2 (the loader's own C, timed with the
  E-clock) puts that at about 3 s of CPU for a k30 kernel, against 11.2 s
  measured in user time.
- `check` hashes two copies of the kernel: the store member, and
  `/stand/k30/unix` against the default's manifest. That is about 2.5 MB
  in 15.1 s, roughly 170 KiB/s against M-SIG2's 540 KiB/s.
- The box binary is GCC 14 output for the 68020 and up, with the 060-safe
  rewrite (D-3 §9). The axtime figures came from the same C built by
  gcc-cross-amix.
- Where the factor of three comes from is not measured yet. It costs
  seconds per commit, not correctness. A timed SHA-256 of a fixed buffer
  in the box binary on the 030 model would separate the compiler from the
  I/O and the copy.

**Notes from the cells.**
- The k30 vehicle's 2 MiB slice holds one raw k30 generation and the
  vehicle's generation 1 only when generation 1 is stored deflated. With
  it raw, 761 856 bytes stay free and gate 5 refuses the next. This
  confirms 40 §2.1: a 1.0-sized slice is small for the lifecycle, and 1.0
  boxes reinstall to the 1.1 layout.
- `s2-standin.sh` wrote no `bss=` for a brel kernel, so gate 2 refused
  until the cell added it. The script now takes the kernel's bss
  (`tests/bench/bss-of.sh` gives it).
- `uname -m` dumps core on the 1.0 K030-U kernel. The cell took the build
  id from `uname -a`'s last field. This matters to the 030 `kinfo` build
  id (S2-11, amix-kerntools).
- The rig's own verdict for the k30 cell is FAIL with the reason `serial`:
  its serial-port shared memory was a version behind ("version mismatch").
  The serial log holds the whole batch's output. The cell is judged on
  that log.

## 3. Verdict

**M-GEN2, bench half, closed:** 16.7 s (median, real) for a k30 commit on
the 25 MHz 68030, of which 11.2 s is CPU, and 16.0 s for its `check`.
Metal (S2-47): the real disk on both boxes, and the real 68060.
